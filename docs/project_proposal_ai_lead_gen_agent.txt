----------------------------------------------

- The orchestrator no longer uses an LLM to route events; routing is

- New "Send Gate" layer: every outbound message is checked for consent,
  open messaging windows, cost and human takeover before it is sent.
- Guardrails added: prices/stock are inserted from the catalog, never
  generated by the LLM; handoff triggers; on-topic-only behaviour.
- New feedback loop: lead quality is sent back to Meta (Conversions API for
  CRM) so ad delivery optimizes for leads that actually buy.
- Success criteria now include business KPIs measured against a baseline.
- New Compliance & Policy section (WhatsApp opt-in rules, Meta AI policy,
  India DPDP Act if selling in India).
- Multi-business (SaaS) use is explicitly deferred to a later phase.


1. BACKGROUND & OBJECTIVE
--------------------------
The stakeholder runs product sales through social media advertising (Instagram,
Facebook, and potentially other platforms). Currently, lead follow-up, comment
responses, and customer engagement are handled manually, which limits response
speed and sales throughput.

Objective: Build a prototype AI agent system that automates lead capture,
qualification, and nurturing end-to-end, using free/local tools first, with an
architecture that can be upgraded to a production-grade deployment later with
minimal rework (swap components, not redesign the system).

Core goal: Increase leads generated and sales closed, while reducing the
salesperson's manual effort to reviewing only pre-qualified, high-intent leads.

Secondary goal: Improve ad efficiency by feeding real lead quality back to
Meta so ad spend is steered toward people who actually buy, not just people
who fill in forms.

Product scope decision: The prototype serves ONE business (the stakeholder).
Turning it into a multi-business product (SaaS) is a separate decision with
its own Meta approval requirements - see Section 6 and the companion spec,
Section 8.


2. SCOPE OF THE PROTOTYPE
--------------------------
In scope:
- Click-to-WhatsApp ad -> instant AI reply on WhatsApp inside the free
  entry point window (primary entry path)
- Lead-form ad capture (with WhatsApp opt-in) -> approved WhatsApp template
  message within 1 minute
- Instagram/Facebook comment monitoring -> one private reply designed to get
  a response -> conversation continues in Instagram/Messenger DM
- Product-grounded responses via RAG; prices and stock always inserted from
  the catalog record, never written by the LLM
- Conversational lead qualification with deterministic scoring
- Lead data stored in a database, mirrored to Google Sheets (CRM stand-in)
- Consent capture and records (opt-in, opt-out, source, timestamp)
- Automated nurture / follow-up sequence using approved templates, for
  opted-in leads only
- Salesperson alerts for hot leads via email / Telegram / dashboard
- Human takeover of any conversation from the dashboard
- Messaging cost tracking per lead and per channel
- Stretch: lead-quality feedback to Meta (Conversions API for CRM) and basic
  ad performance visibility

Out of scope for prototype (planned for later phases):
- Onboarding other businesses (multi-tenant SaaS, Meta Tech Provider /
  Embedded Signup, Meta App Review for advanced access)
- Full CRM (HubSpot/Zoho) integration
- Multi-rep team routing and permissions
- Paid hosted LLM API (Ollama used locally for prototype)
- Voice-note transcription (voice notes trigger a human handoff in the
  prototype)
- High-volume template campaigns / broadcasts


3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE (MULTI-AGENT)
--------------------------------------
Router (formerly "Orchestrator Agent")
   - Central entry point. Receives all inbound events (CTWA message, form
     fill, comment, Instagram DM, WhatsApp reply, delivery status) and
     routes them to the correct specialist agent.
   - Routing is deterministic code, NOT an LLM call: the webhook endpoint
     and event type already identify what happened. This removes latency
     and a failure point. LLM calls happen only inside the agents, for
     classification, extraction and reply writing.
   - Every webhook is signature-verified, acknowledged immediately, queued,
     and de-duplicated before routing (details in the spec).

Specialist Agents (prototype = Python modules; production = separate
microservices):

   a) Capture Agent
      - Normalizes incoming lead data (name, contact, product interest,
        source, ad/campaign IDs, consent) into one consistent record
        regardless of channel.
      - Records attribution identifiers (Meta lead ID, ad ID, campaign ID,
        CTWA click ID) needed for the Meta feedback loop.

   b) Engage Agent (comments and Instagram/Messenger DMs)
      - Classifies comment intent (lead signal / question / complaint /
        spam / unrelated)
      - Sends ONE Instagram/Facebook private reply within Meta's 7-day
        window. Because only one message is allowed until the person
        replies, that message must ask a question or give a clear call to
        action (e.g. "Which size are you looking for? I can send prices").
      - Optionally posts a short public reply ("Sent you a DM") and hides
        spam.
      - Continues the conversation in Instagram/Messenger DM once the person
        replies (24-hour messaging window), or invites them to WhatsApp.
      - Uses RAG to answer product questions grounded in real catalog data.

   c) Qualify Agent
      - Runs a conversational flow to learn budget, timeline, product and
        quantity, mixed naturally with answering the lead's questions
        rather than reading out a form.
      - Asks for several details in one message where natural: every
        outbound WhatsApp message now costs money, so fewer, denser
        messages are preferred.
      - The LLM extracts answers into structured fields (JSON); a fixed
        scoring rubric in code turns them into a score and Hot / Warm / Cold
        status, with the reasoning stored.
      - Hot leads trigger an immediate alert to the salesperson via email,
        Telegram or dashboard push (not WhatsApp, which would need a paid
        template).

   d) Nurture Agent
      - Scheduled job that checks for inactive leads (no reply in N days)
      - Sends follow-ups only to leads with recorded WhatsApp opt-in, using
        pre-approved template messages (required once the 24-hour window is
        closed; marketing templates are always charged outside the free
        entry window).
      - Caps the number of follow-ups per lead and stops on reply,
        conversion, opt-out, or human takeover.

Send Gate (new, shared by all agents)
   - Every outbound message passes through one function that:
       * checks opt-in / opt-out status
       * checks which messaging window is open (24-hour service window,
         72-hour free entry window, Instagram 24-hour window)
       * chooses free-form vs template accordingly, or blocks the send
       * blocks automated sends while a human has taken over the lead
       * runs guardrail checks (prices/stock match the catalog)
       * logs the message and its estimated cost
   - This keeps messaging rules and costs in one place instead of being
     re-implemented in every agent.

Guardrails
   - Prices, stock levels and offers are filled in from the catalog record
     by code; any number in an LLM reply is validated against retrieved
     product data before sending.
   - The agent stays on the business's products and declines unrelated
     requests (also required by WhatsApp's 2026 policy on AI assistants -
     see Section 11).
   - Customer messages are treated as untrusted input (prompt-injection
     resistant prompts; no tool actions triggered directly by user text).
   - Handoff to the salesperson when: the customer asks for a human, is
     angry or complaining, sends a voice note or image the agent cannot
     handle, or the agent's confidence is low.

Data Layer
   - Database (SQLite for the prototype, Postgres later) is the system of
     record for leads, conversations, consent, conversation state and
     message costs.
   - Google Sheets is a read-only mirror of the lead table so the
     salesperson can browse leads in a familiar tool. Column design still
     maps directly onto future CRM fields.

Feedback Loop to Meta (stretch)
   - When a lead becomes qualified or converted, that stage is sent to Meta
     via the Conversions API for CRM, keyed on the Meta lead ID. Meta can
     then optimize lead ads for "conversion leads" (people likely to buy)
     instead of raw form fills. This is the most direct link between the
     system and more sales per rupee of ad spend.


4. HOW PRODUCT DETAILS ARE SOURCED (RAG PIPELINE)
----------------------------------------------------
1. Product catalog maintained as a structured file (JSON/CSV to start), with
   price, stock and key attributes as explicit fields.
2. Product descriptions embedded with a local embedding model. If customers
   write in Indian languages, Hinglish or transliterated text, use a
   multilingual model (e.g. bge-m3) rather than an English-centred one
   (e.g. nomic-embed-text); choose using the evaluation set (Section 9).
3. Embeddings stored in a local vector store (ChromaDB, or pgvector inside
   the same Postgres database to reduce moving parts).
4. Retrieval is hybrid: structured filters (category, price range, in
   stock) plus vector similarity. For a small catalog (roughly under 100
   products) the whole catalog summary can also be placed in the prompt.
5. Retrieved entries are passed into the LLM prompt so replies are grounded
   in actual product data. Prices and stock are inserted from the record by
   code, not copied by the model.
6. Updating a product = updating the source and re-embedding that product
   only; no retraining required.

This RAG design is model-agnostic: the retrieval pipeline does not change when
Ollama is later replaced with a hosted LLM API (Claude, GPT, etc.) - only the
generation call changes.


5. TECH STACK - PROTOTYPE (FREE / LOW-COST FIRST)
--------------------------------------------------
LLM (local):              Ollama; model chosen by evaluation (candidates:
                           Llama 3.x, Qwen, Gemma - test language support
                           with real customer messages). Structured JSON
                           output for all classification/extraction calls.
Embeddings (local):        Multilingual embedding model via Ollama or
                           sentence-transformers (e.g. bge-m3)
Vector store:              ChromaDB, or pgvector
Database:                  SQLite (prototype) -> Postgres
Sheets mirror:             Google Sheets API (free tier), read-only view
Backend / webhook server:  Python FastAPI with a background task queue
Scheduler (nurture jobs):  APScheduler
Social integration:        Meta Graph API (comments, private replies,
                           Instagram/Messenger messaging, lead forms) -
                           requires Meta Developer App + Business
                           verification
Messaging:                 WhatsApp Cloud API (see Section 7 for costs)
Salesperson alerts:        Email (SMTP) and/or Telegram bot, plus dashboard
                           WebSocket push
Hosting (prototype):       One always-on machine running both the backend
                           and Ollama (a low-cost VM, ideally with a GPU, or
                           a dedicated local machine with a static ngrok
                           domain). Avoid free hosting tiers that sleep when
                           idle: a sleeping server can take about a minute
                           to wake, which breaks the 1-minute response goal
                           and causes Meta webhook retries.


6. MIGRATION PATH TO INDUSTRIAL DEPLOYMENT
----------------------------------------------
Designed so each swap is a component replacement, not a redesign:

  Prototype component            ->  Production replacement
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Ollama (local LLM)             ->  Hosted LLM API (Claude/GPT/etc.)
  SQLite                         ->  Managed Postgres
  ChromaDB / pgvector            ->  pgvector or managed vector DB
                                      (Pinecone/Weaviate) if scale needs
  Google Sheets mirror           ->  Full CRM (HubSpot/Zoho/Salesforce)
  In-process background tasks    ->  Message queue + separate workers
  Single always-on machine       ->  Cloud hosting with autoscaling
  Router + agent modules         ->  Separate microservices per agent
  Manual scoring rubric          ->  Tuned scoring model trained on
                                      accumulated lead outcomes
  Single business                ->  Multi-tenant SaaS (requires Meta
                                      App Review, Tech Provider status and
                                      Embedded Signup for WhatsApp)

Because the RAG pipeline, agent interfaces, Send Gate and data schema are
defined the same way from day one (including a user_id column on every
table), this migration is mostly infrastructure swaps and configuration.


7. COST CONSIDERATIONS
------------------------
Software: Ollama, ChromaDB/pgvector, SQLite/Postgres, Google Sheets API and
the Meta Graph API are free at prototype scale. Instagram/Messenger
messaging has no per-message fee.

Hosting: an always-on machine that can run a local LLM is a real monthly
cost (small VM or dedicated hardware). Budget for it from Week 1.

WhatsApp (current rules, verify against Meta's rate card before
budgeting):
- Meta charges per delivered message, not per conversation (since July
  2025).
- Since 1 October 2026, free-form replies inside the 24-hour customer
  service window, and utility templates sent inside that window, are
  charged per message. Previously these were free. Some providers report a
  monthly free allowance of service messages per business number - confirm
  with Meta/BSP.
- Still free: all messages inside the 72-hour free entry point window that
  opens when a customer messages you from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad and you
  reply within 24 hours.
- Marketing templates (lead-form first message, nurture follow-ups) are
  always charged outside the free entry window. In India the 2026 marketing
  rate is roughly Rs 0.86 per delivered message, before BSP markup and GST.
- A BSP (Wati, Gupshup, Twilio, etc.), if used, adds its own markup.

Design consequences:
- Make CTWA ads the main WhatsApp entry point and reply within seconds.
- Keep conversations short and information-dense; every agent reply in the
  24-hour window is now billable.
- Track estimated messaging cost per lead from day one (the Send Gate logs
  it), so the stakeholder can see cost per qualified lead.
- Agree a monthly messaging budget before real ad traffic starts.

Rough cost formula for planning:
  monthly WhatsApp cost ~=
      (lead-form leads x first template)
    + (nurture templates sent)
    + (agent replies outside free entry windows x service rate)
    - (monthly free allowance, if any)


8. PROTOTYPE PHASES
----------------------
Phase 0 (start immediately, runs in parallel): Platform approvals
  - Meta Business verification, WhatsApp number registration and display
    name approval, message template submission (lead-form first message,
    nurture follow-ups), opt-in wording on lead forms
  - Capture baseline metrics (current response time, reply rate,
    lead-to-sale rate) before automation goes live
  - Collect 50-100 real past comments/messages for the evaluation set

Phase 1: Capture + instant response
  - CTWA ad -> WhatsApp message -> Capture Agent -> RAG reply in free window
  - Lead form webhook -> Capture Agent -> approved WhatsApp template

Phase 2: Comment-to-lead
  - Comment webhook -> intent classification -> one private reply ->
    Instagram/Messenger DM conversation -> Capture Agent

Phase 3: Qualification + alerts
  - Qualify flow -> structured extraction -> scoring rubric -> hot-lead
    alert (email/Telegram/dashboard)
  - Lead table mirrored to Google Sheets

Phase 4: Dashboard + human takeover
  - Lead inbox, lead detail, takeover/release, live notifications

Phase 5: Nurture sequence
  - Scheduled template follow-ups for opted-in inactive leads, with caps
    and opt-out handling

Phase 6 (stretch): Ad feedback loop
  - Send qualified/converted stages to Meta via Conversions API for CRM
  - Pull Meta Ads performance data to show which ads produce qualified
    leads and at what messaging + ad cost


9. SUCCESS CRITERIA FOR PROTOTYPE
--------------------------------------
Technical (pass/fail):
- End-to-end flow works for one product category across three entry paths:
  CTWA ad, lead form, and comment
- First response within 1 minute of the lead's action (95% of cases)
- Lead data logged with no manual entry; Sheets mirror stays in sync
- No reply sent with a price or stock figure that differs from the catalog
- No duplicate messages from webhook retries
- Recorded opt-in exists for 100% of template messages sent
- Nurture sequence re-engages at least one test lead with no manual step

AI quality (measured on the evaluation set; proposed targets, to be agreed):
- Comment intent classification accuracy >= 90%
- Product questions answered correctly (or correctly handed off) >= 95%
- Salesperson agrees with "Hot" labels for >= 80% of hot leads

Business (measured against the Phase 0 baseline):
- Median time to first response
- Lead reply rate
- Cost per qualified lead (ad spend + messaging)
- Lead-to-sale conversion rate
- Salesperson hours spent per closed sale


10. OPEN QUESTIONS FOR STAKEHOLDER SIGN-OFF
------------------------------------------------
- Is this for one business only, or intended to become a product for other
  businesses? (Changes Meta approval requirements and scope.)
- Product catalog source/format for the RAG knowledge base
- Qualifying questions and scoring priorities (what makes a lead "hot")
- WhatsApp number and Meta Business account availability; who will own
  template approval
- Which languages customers write in (affects model and embedding choice);
  how common voice notes are
- Expected monthly lead volume and acceptable monthly messaging budget
- Willingness to run Click-to-WhatsApp ads alongside or instead of lead
  forms
- Whether lead volume is around 200+ per month, which Meta suggests for the
  Conversion Leads optimization goal
- Baseline data available today (response times, conversion rate)


11. COMPLIANCE & PLATFORM POLICY
------------------------------------
WhatsApp
- Business-initiated messages (templates) require the customer's opt-in.
  Lead forms must include clear WhatsApp consent wording; consent source
  and timestamp are stored per lead.
- Opt-out keywords (e.g. STOP, in the languages customers use) and
  template opt-out buttons are honoured immediately.
- Since 15 January 2026 Meta prohibits general-purpose AI assistants on the
  WhatsApp Business Platform. Business-specific sales and support agents
  remain allowed; this system must stay focused on the business's own
  products and services.

Instagram / Facebook
- One private reply per comment, within 7 days of the comment.
- After a person messages the business, replies are allowed within a
  24-hour window.
- Request only the permissions the system actually uses.

Data protection (if the business sells in India)
- The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 are being phased in;
  the main notice, consent and breach obligations apply from 14 May 2027.
- Build now: a clear consent notice, consent records, data minimisation,
  the ability to delete a lead's data on request, and breach handling.
- Keep personal data out of application logs; restrict who can open the
  Sheets mirror.
